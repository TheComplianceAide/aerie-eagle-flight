Mendenhall Glacier, Alaska
Ice from the lower Mendenhall uses a rectified crop of Aaron Jacobs's photograph for the National Weather Service, released by the USGS Alaska Science Center on July 21, 2011. The USGS marks the photograph public domain.

Horseshoe Bend, Arizona
Horseshoe Bend 001, photographed October 30, 2017 by Brent and Dawn Davis for the National Park Service, Glen Canyon National Recreation Area. NPGallery records full public-domain rights. The dry sandstone faces were registered to the surveyed canyon. A separate crop of the foreground provides compacted sandstone and surface grains near the east overlook. The river, sky, plants and sunlight are drawn in the game.

Crater Lake, Oregon
USGS 3D Elevation Program bare-earth terrain, Oregon SouthEast D22 project. Two one-metre tiles, 10 x56y476 and x57y476, cover the new terrain centered on Wizard Island and the western caldera. USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program (NAIP), acquired September 23, 2022 at 0.3 m, supplied the red, near-infrared and visible surface measurements. Crown centers, coarsened rock colors and sky visibility are derived from these sources. Tree heights, individual lava grains and storm weather are simulated. The USGS field photograph by Charles R. Bacon in Scientific Investigations Map 2832 (2008) provided a visual reference.

Yosemite Valley, California
USGS National Hydrography Dataset and USDA NAIP imagery. The August 7, 2022 flight at 0.6 m supplies red and near-infrared signatures used to estimate exposed gravel and both banks of the Merced River. Water beneath dense tree cover is interpolated. Distant reflections are sampled from regional USGS terrain and previously credited canopy measurements. Water stage and velocity are simulated.

Nugget Falls and Mendenhall Lake
Topography comes from the USGS 2022–2023 Southeast Alaska Landslides 0.5 m bare-earth survey. The local reconstruction uses 1 m samples around the chute and aligns the lake plane to the established game datum. Crown positions, heights and widths are estimates from the 2012 Juneau lidar published by USGS and the City and Borough of Juneau 2013 lidar distributed by NOAA Digital Coast. Colorized first returns recover the 2013 regional aerial mosaic over the lower falls, exposed metamorphic shelves, sandbar and forest. The 2013 active whitewater limits the live channel where observations exist. Farther upstream the course follows the existing modeled trace. Crown species and discharge are inferred. Color outside the 2013 footprint also uses modified Copernicus Sentinel-2 data acquired August 8, 2024.

Yosemite's valley walls and eastern skyline
The National Park Service Tunnel View image by C. Jacoby supplies only the photographed dry-rock areas of the intervening valley walls and buttresses. Ralph H. Anderson's “Half Dome from Glacier Pt.,” October 6, 1938, Yosemite Historic Photo Collection negative RL_00603 (catalogued as public domain), supplies Half Dome's visible visor and south wall. NPS geologist Greg Stock's granite specimen, figure 19 of the 2012 Yosemite National Park Geologic Resources Inventory Report, supplies fine mineral grains. Color and relief in the distant Half Dome–Clouds Rest mesh come from the USDA California August 20, 2022 60 cm NAIP survey and the USGS 1/3 arcsecond n38w120 DEM, updated May 2025 from 2017–2023 source data. The DEM is projected onto an 8 m grid. The sun, atmosphere, water, and the granite's present-day illumination are simulated.
