AERIE CALL TIMING

How often each animal in the game is heard, and what it does when the eagle
comes close. The numbers are in game/field-audio.js (the timing table). Each
entry below gives what the game uses, then what was found, in the researcher's
own tags: [measured] a figure from a study, [described] a qualitative account,
[derived] arithmetic on measured figures, [estimate] a rough figure where no
measurement was found. Where nothing was found the game's value is a judgement
and says so. Bald-eagle-specific call counts per flyover were found for no
species; the eagle responses rest on accounts of behaviour toward eagles and
other large raptors.

AMERICAN BLACK DUCK  (black-duck)
Game: undisturbed, a bout every 90 to 240 s; at the eagle: calls on 80% of encounters, 1 to 2 recordings a bout, up to 1 bout.
Undisturbed: [described] Females give loud quacks and a six-syllable call that
  falls in pitch (accent on the second syllable); males give raspy, reedy
  calls and a flutelike whistle in courtship (All About Birds). Rate: not
  found. [interpolated from mallard, a very close relative] treat as mallard:
  occasional 5-6 note decrescendos, long quiet spells.
Raptors: not found for this species. [interpolated from dabbling ducks
  generally] more vigilance when eagles are around; flush when an eagle
  approaches closely.
Confidence: low

WOOD DUCK  (wood-duck)
Game: undisturbed, a bout every 300 to 900 s; at the eagle: calls on 85% of encounters, 1 to 2 recordings a bout, up to 1 bout.
Undisturbed: [described] Quiet unless disturbed. Male: thin, rising-and-
  falling 'zeeting' whistle ('jeee'); female contact calls keep the pair
  together in winter and spring (BirdNote). Rate: not found.
Raptors: [described] Female gives a loud, repeated 'oo-eek, oo-eek' when
  disturbed and taking flight (All About Birds); this is the flush call for
  any disturbance. Eagle-specific data: not found.
Confidence: low

COMMON MERGANSER  (merganser)
Game: silent unless disturbed; at the eagle: calls on 40% of encounters, 1 recording a bout, up to 1 bout.
Undisturbed: [described] 'Usually silent' (All About Birds). Females give a
  high rapid 'cro cro cro' only to call ducklings from the nest; males give
  bell-like or twanging calls only in courtship. Daytime rate when undisturbed
  is effectively zero.
Raptors: [described] Females give harsh 'gruk' calls when threatened by
  predators; males give hoarse calls when alarmed (All About Birds). As a
  diving duck it tends to stay on the water and dive when attacked. Counts and
  eagle-specific data: not found.
Confidence: medium (that it is normally silent); low (alarm detail)
https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Common_Merganser/sounds
https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Mergus_merganser/

COMMON EIDER  (eider)
Game: undisturbed, a bout every 90 to 240 s; at the eagle: calls on 50% of encounters, 1 to 2 recordings a bout, up to 1 bout.
Undisturbed: [described] Males are vocal in the courtship season (late winter-
  spring): soft cooing calls that carry far over calm water; females give a
  low repeated 'gog-gog-gog' when feeding or answering displays (All About
  Birds). Rate: not found. [estimate] outside courtship, rafts are
  largely quiet.
Raptors: [described] Harsh grating 'kor-korr-korr', probably an alarm call,
  from both sexes (All About Birds). Creche-tending females watch the sky and
  signal ducklings to dive when eagles approach (Maine coast natural-history
  account); bald eagles attack adult eiders on the water (Great Duck Island
  observations). In the Baltic, white-tailed eagle predation on incubating
  females and ducklings reduced risk-taking and shifted breeding islands
  (Mohring, Abo Akademi thesis). Call counts: not found.
Confidence: low

PARADISE SHELDUCK  (shelduck)
Game: undisturbed, a bout every 60 to 150 s; at the eagle: calls on 100% of encounters, 2 to 4 recordings a bout, up to 3 bouts 8 to 18 s apart.
Undisturbed: [described] Male: two-syllable goose-like honk ('zonk-zonk')
  given when alarmed or in flight. Female: shriller, more rapid and more
  persistent call ('zeek-zeek'), especially in flight (NZ Birds Online). Pairs
  stay close and call in turn as they move about (Te Ara). Rate: not found.
Raptors: [described] Alarmed pairs flee calling persistently, female most
  prominent; parents use a broken-wing display against ground predators.
  Response to harrier or falcon specifically: not found. (New Zealand has no
  eagle; the swamp harrier is the usual aerial threat.)
Confidence: low

BELTED KINGFISHER  (kingfisher)
Game: undisturbed, a bout every 120 to 300 s; at the eagle: calls on 90% of encounters, 1 to 2 recordings a bout, up to 1 bout.
Undisturbed: [measured, nest context only] Adults rattled on 92.7% of 109 nest
  approaches and 56.2% of 105 nest departures (Wilson J. Ornithol. study, doi
  10.1676/19-00099). [described] Strident mechanical rattle given 'in response
  to the slightest disturbance' and in territorial disputes; usually heard as
  the bird flies along the shore. Rattles per hour: not found.
Raptors: [described] Rattles at any disturbance; screams (males add harsh
  calls) when threatened (All About Birds); escapes predators such as hawks by
  diving into the water (Pennsylvania Game Commission). Eagle-specific: not
  found.
Confidence: low

AMERICAN DIPPER  (dipper)
Game: undisturbed, a bout every 60 to 180 s; at the eagle: calls on 40% of encounters, 1 recording a bout, up to 1 bout.

  series in flight. Bouts per hour: not found.

COMMON GULL  (common-gull)
Game: undisturbed, a bout every 30 to 90 s; at the eagle: calls on 100% of encounters, 2 to 4 recordings a bout, up to 3 bouts 6 to 14 s apart.
Undisturbed: [described] Largely silent through the winter (BTO). At breeding
  sites: high, scratchy, squealing 'ka', 'kyew', 'kia' calls and a display
  long call (All About Birds account of the North American sister form, Short-
  billed/Mew Gull). Rate: not found.
Raptors: [described from annotated recordings, no counts] 'Kia / kiaa-kiaa'
  alarm calls; recordings exist of alarm calling at an osprey, of calls given
  in response to a white-tailed eagle over a loose colony, and of adults
  mobbing at a colony edge (xeno-canto / Macaulay Library notes).
Confidence: low

ARCTIC TERN  (tern)
Game: undisturbed, a bout every 15 to 40 s; at the eagle: calls on 100% of encounters, 2 to 4 recordings a bout, up to 3 bouts 6 to 14 s apart.

  (Greenland colony study); 'dreads' (sudden mass upflights, often false

BLACK-LEGGED KITTIWAKE  (kittiwake)
Game: undisturbed, a bout every 12 to 30 s; at the eagle: calls on 90% of encounters, 2 to 3 recordings a bout, up to 2 bouts 8 to 16 s apart.
Undisturbed: [described] A nasal, repeated 'kittiwake' is the most commonly
  heard call on the breeding grounds, plus short nasal calls between mates and
  to chicks (All About Birds). Rate: not found. [estimate] an occupied
  cliff is a continuous chorus; birds at sea are quiet.
Raptors: [described; escape response scored in a thesis] Eagle overflights
  make adults leave nests in mass panic flights; the escape response (empty
  nests) was stronger when white-tailed eagles were present (Hornoya, UiT
  thesis); USGS documents bald eagles disturbing nesting kittiwakes; rising
  eagle numbers sped up the loss of an exposed colony at Rost, Norway (42-year
  study). No distinct alarm call is described. Flush duration and time to
  return: not found.
Confidence: medium (flush behaviour); low (timing)

COMMON RAVEN  (raven)
Game: undisturbed, a bout every 120 to 360 s; at the eagle: calls on 85% of encounters, 1 to 3 recordings a bout, up to 2 bouts 15 to 35 s apart.
Undisturbed: not found as a rate. [described] Very varied repertoire (up to 33
  call categories); the usual call is a deep gurgling croak (All About Birds).
  [estimate] a territorial pair gives a few croaks every few minutes,
  often in flight, with long silent gaps.
Raptors: [described] 'Short, repeated, shrill calls when chasing predators or
  trespassers' and deep rasping calls when the nest is disturbed (All About
  Birds). Ravens readily mob and chase eagles: groups pursue passing golden
  eagles at Hawk Mountain; a group mobbed a golden eagle that had caught a
  raven for about 5 min (J. Raptor Res. 16:134-136).
Confidence: medium (mobs eagles, qualitative); low (rate)

STELLER'S JAY  (jay)
Game: undisturbed, a bout every 60 to 160 s; at the eagle: calls on 90% of encounters, 2 to 3 recordings a bout, up to 2 bouts 10 to 25 s apart.
Undisturbed: [described] Loud, repeated 'shook shook shook shook' given year-
  round in flight, perched and in aggressive encounters; also guttural notes,
  a harsh nasal growl, mimicry of hawks; quiet whisper song in courtship (All
  About Birds). Rate: not found.
Raptors: [measured single observation plus experiments] 'Wah' is the mobbing
  call: 13 s after a perched Cooper's Hawk called, one jay gave a 'wah';
  within 7 s, 5-6 jays were mobbing the hawk in a chorus of 'wahs' (Ficken, J.
  Field Ornithol. 60:52-55). 'Wah-wah-wah' calls are loud, harsh, wide-band
  and are given to a stationary (perched) raptor; jays change alarm-call
  structure with raptor species and with whether they saw or heard it
  (Billings et al., Dryad data). For songbirds generally a raptor in flight
  draws high 'seet'...
Confidence: medium

PEREGRINE FALCON  (peregrine)
Game: silent unless disturbed; at the eagle: calls on 90% of encounters, 2 to 3 recordings a bout, up to 3 bouts 6 to 15 s apart.

Game: silent unless disturbed; at the eagle: calls on 90% of encounters, 1 recording a bout, up to 1 bout.
Undisturbed: [described] Silent while foraging; the whistle is an alarm signal

  last up to nine months at altitude.

  100:1-13): marmots were more likely to give a SINGLE-note whistle as their
