Terrain, plants and water
USGS 3D Elevation Program, Yellowstone National Park 2020 survey (WY_YellowstoneNP_2020_D20).
The public 1 m bare-earth tiles supply terrain. Point returns from the same survey support sampled
canopy positions and crown heights. Display meshes sample those grids at several spacings.
https://apps.nationalmap.gov/downloader/
https://usgs-lidar-public.s3.amazonaws.com/WY_YellowstoneNP_3_2020/ept.json

USDA National Agriculture Imagery Program, Wyoming July 2022 aerial photography.
The 60 cm target boxes follow individual basins. Surroundings use lower-resolution exports.
The spring contours follow the visible water. Rendered water, sediments and steam move in the game.

Four geyser photogrammetry surveys
Yellowstone National Park / Yellowstone Volcano Observatory, Old Faithful, Castle, Giant and
Lone Star, 2019 field capture. Original scan geometry is registered to the terrain. Castle and
Lone Star were simplified for the browser. Lone Star’s original STL is untextured. The local
stone material is used where a scan has no color.

Sky and hydrothermal optics
Evening Road 01 (Pure Sky), Sergej Majboroda and Jarod Guest, Poly Haven, CC0.
Warm-lit pale sinter and spring color appearance informed by Nugent, Shaw et al., Applied Optics 2015.
https://polyhaven.com/a/evening_road_01_puresky
https://www.montana.edu/jshaw/documents/YNP%20pool%20colors%20-%20Nugent%20et%20al%20-%20AO2015.pdf

The landscape shows a July 2022 surface snapshot, including Biscuit Basin before its 2024
hydrothermal explosion. The artwork and flight timings depict an interpretation of a late
summer afternoon. Individual tree species are assigned from regional vegetation. Pool depth
and bank gravel are reconstructed. No live observation is implied.
