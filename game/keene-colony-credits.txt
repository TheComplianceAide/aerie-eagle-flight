COLONY’S BLOCK
4–7 Central Square, Keene, New Hampshire. The original Second Empire block dates to 1870. The street-facing wall was registered on the eastern side of Central Square. The simulation includes three differently profiled mansards, recessed sash and entrances, a central recessed bay, projecting stone courses and the fifth-floor dormers.

Aerie rectified and color-balanced the brick and sash, reduced tree branches, derived a normal map, isolated each roof front, and rebuilt slate fields from unobstructed surfaces under the projecting dormers, and used the visible upper bays of each adjoining building on its own wing. The adjoining upper fronts repeat those visible bays. The pale lettering of the original central building tablet is part of the photograph. Storefront glass and joinery below the upper facade are an original approximation. The photograph records 2012 conditions.

Geometry and register
The 1982 National Register nomination and archive images identify the original 63.7-foot frontage, facade divisions, Vermont slate pattern, bracketed cornice, dormers, sandstone bands and rear additions. The City of Keene's downtown inventory provides its civic context. These archival images informed shape and scale. The archival photographs were used as shape references.
