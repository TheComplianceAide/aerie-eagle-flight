COLONY’S BLOCK
4–7 Central Square, Keene, New Hampshire. The original Second Empire block dates to 1870. The street-facing wall was registered on the eastern side of Central Square. The simulation includes three differently profiled mansards, recessed sash and entrances, a central recessed bay, projecting stone courses and the fifth-floor dormers.

Geometry and register
The 1982 National Register nomination and archive images identify the original 63.7-foot frontage, facade divisions, Vermont slate pattern, bracketed cornice, dormers, sandstone bands and rear additions. The City of Keene's downtown inventory provides its civic context. These archival images informed shape and scale. The archival photographs were used as shape references.

CENTRAL SQUARE TERRACE FOOTWAY — field evidence and release credit
Four to seven Central Square, Keene, NH. Registered state orthophotography shows the continuous pedestrian frontage behind the angled on-street parking. The local ground survey places the inside edge of the earlier modeled sidewalk about 3.7 m from the row of shops. Aerie connected that edge to the modeled door sills, preserving the existing outer sidewalk outline, street, angled parking lines and cars. Raised granite on the existing line now has a physical roadside face. The existing Keene concrete scan supplies the surface. The slab joints, stone divisions, intermediate grade and worn concrete finish are interpretations at a scale below the aerial reference. The 2012 street photograph corroborates the retail frontage and parking; cars obscure much of the pavement. No source-image pixels are used by this addition.
