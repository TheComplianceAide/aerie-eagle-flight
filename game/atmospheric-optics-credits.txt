AERIE · ATMOSPHERIC OPTICS

These events are original artistic simulations of naturally occurring optical
phenomena. They are placed in each world's selected weather and time of day.
They do not recreate a dated meteorological observation in any of the parks.

Alaska, Oeschinensee, Lofoten and Winnipesaukee
High cirrus can contain hexagonal plates and crystals. Rays refracted through
them can make a solar halo with a roughly 22° inner radius, or bright
parhelia near the Sun's elevation with a red sunward edge and a whitish tail.
Reflections off horizontal plates can produce a pillar over a low Sun.
U.S. National Weather Service:
https://www.weather.gov/arx/why_halos_sundogs_pillars
Hong Kong Observatory, parhelion geometry:
https://www.weather.gov.hk/en/education/earth-science/optical-phenomena/00353-indepth-anatomy-of-the-atmospheric-optical-phenomenon-sun-dog.html

Dixville, Moosehead, Surry, Yosemite and Horseshoe Bend
Mountains and broken clouds interrupt direct sunlight. Areas lit through
gaps scatter light toward an observer in haze, dust or droplets. Parallel
rays appear to converge near the solar or antisolar point. Four finite
ellipsoids approximate illuminated air; their optical path ends at the visible
terrain, canopy or water surface.
World Meteorological Organization, International Cloud Atlas:
https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/crepuscular-rays.html
UK Met Office, optical effects:
https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/optical-effects

Lac Berté
Light refracted and reflected by droplets makes the original rainbows.
Fine, relatively uniform droplets can give muted interference fringes just
inside the primary. Only part of the existing game shower supports them.
World Meteorological Organization, International Cloud Atlas:
https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/rainbow.html

Crater Lake
Intracloud lightning illuminates a region inside a storm cloud. The simulation
lets the cloud itself diffuse and obscure the light.
NOAA National Severe Storms Laboratory:
https://www.nssl.noaa.gov/education/svrwx101/lightning/types/

Acadia National Park
The low green-blue band above the Atlantic represents atmospheric airglow. The fixed stellar field and Galactic dust lanes follow NASA SVS Deep Star Maps 2020 at the latitude of Mount Desert Island.
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4851/
