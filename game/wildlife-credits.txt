AERIE · REGIONAL LAND WILDLIFE

These are representative encounters placed on the game's terrain. The animal
species are documented in the relevant regions. Exact animals, group sizes,
sightings, movement, times and approach positions are artistic simulations.

SPECIES AND HABITATS

Moosehead Lake, Maine
Maine Department of Agriculture, Conservation and Forestry, Moosehead Region
Management Plan. The Little Moose Unit supports moose, white-tailed deer and
snowshoe hare. Maine Department of Inland Fisheries and Wildlife describes
moose proportions, antlers, neck bell and feeding at wet forest margins.
The snowshoe hare wears its brown summer coat in the game.
https://www.maine.gov/dacf/parks/get_involved/planning_and_acquisition/management_plans/docs/MooseheadMgmtPlan-FINAL%20DRAFT_4-7-16-part1.pdf
https://www.maine.gov/ifw/fish-wildlife/wildlife/species-information/mammals/moose.html
https://www.maine.gov/ifw/fish-wildlife/wildlife/species-information/mammals/cottontail-snowshoe-hare.html

Lake Winnipesaukee, Surry Mountain Lake and Dixville Notch, New Hampshire
New Hampshire Fish and Game documents white-tailed deer, moose and snowshoe
hare in New Hampshire. Moose are featured at the northern Dixville site. Deer
and snowshoe hare occupy forest margins in the two more southerly lake worlds.

The mapped tributary also hosts spawning adult sockeye near its viewing platforms.

MODELS AND MATERIALS

Mammal bodies, face markings, distinct antlers and horns, limb articulation,
movement and spatial placement are original game models. The shared close fur
detail in wild-mammal-coat.webp is derived from Bicanski's photograph “fur,
rabbit,” CC0 1.0. It was cropped, rotated, desaturated, high-pass filtered and
resized. Anatomical markings and colour differ by species.
https://pixnio.com/textures-and-patterns/fur/fur-of-rabbit
https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/
The Lofoten otter's wave-washed ledge is original relief with the game's
existing photographed shore-stone material, attributed in credits.txt.

Acadia National Park, Maine — late-summer Atlantic shore and spruce woods
Common eiders feed and paddle in Newport Cove. Harbor seals rest on low coastal ledges. White-tailed deer and snowshoe hares live on wooded slopes. Barred owls hunt at night. Regional animal patch

The osprey, double-crested cormorant, and killdeer use original drawn body
profiles generated by tools/fauna/build_animal_patch.py. There are no outside
photo pixels in those textures. The shared bird renderer animates the wings;
these are gameplay illustrations, rather than scanned specimens.

The North American river otter, American beaver, muskrat, red fox, raccoon,
coyote, mountain goat, chamois, and mountain hare extend the game's original
articulated mammal rig. Their geometry and colors are authored for this patch.
Freshwater mammals can forage on the bank. This patch has no swimming rig or
new recordings for those mammals.

Osprey encounters reuse the edited CC0 Pandion haliaetus excerpt credited as
voice-labrador-1147350.m4a in regional-recording-credits.txt. Previously
credited raptor, waterfowl, heron, kingfisher, tern, nutcracker and squirrel
recordings use the existing field-audio credits. New call positions require
the matching runtime actor. The Yellowstone killdeer recording remains on its
existing Geyser Hill soundscape. The cormorants do not emit a new recording.

Follow-up animal patch

The additional grouse, swallow, woodpecker, hawk, turkey, condor, gulls,
vultures, guillemot, shag, ptarmigan, and gray seal use original illustrated
profiles built by the same generator. The woodland caribou, marmot, and
ground squirrels use variants of the articulated mammal rig. Their homes
are simulated habitat placements. They do not mark documented individuals,
nests, or haul-outs.

The condor silhouette and cliff-roost association refer to the National Park
Service's California condor species description and Glen Canyon account.
https://www.nps.gov/glca/learn/nature/condors.htm
https://home.nps.gov/articles/california-condor.htm

The Broad-winged Hawk uses the existing edited CC0 excerpt credited as
voice-dixville-2107726.m4a in regional-recording-credits.txt. No recording
was imported for the other follow-up species.
